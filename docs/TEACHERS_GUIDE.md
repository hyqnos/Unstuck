# Unstuck — A Guide for Teachers & Support Staff

A short, plain guide for anyone introducing **Unstuck** to students. No jargon, no setup headaches.

---

## What it is

Unstuck is a **thinking space** — a place a student puts what's in their head so they don't have to hold it all at once. Thoughts, tasks, reminders, half-ideas: type them in, and they land on a calm map the student can see and come back to.

It is **not** a productivity tracker, a planner you check up on, or a grade book. It's a tool a student uses *for themselves*.

**Who it helps:** any student who gets overwhelmed by long lists, loses the thread mid-task, forgets why they walked into a room, or freezes when there's "too much." That's a lot of students — and this guide never assumes a diagnosis. There are no labels anywhere in the app, by design.

---

## The one thing to understand: it's private to the student

This matters most, so it's first:

- **Everything stays on the student's own device.** Nothing syncs to a cloud, a server, a school system, or to you. There is no dashboard, no teacher view, no report.
- **You cannot see what a student writes in it, and that's the point.** It only works because it's a safe, private place. Please introduce it that way and don't ask to see inside it.
- **No account, no sign-up, no email.** Nothing to collect, nothing to leak.

If your school has a data-privacy review, the short version is: *on-device only, collects nothing, transmits nothing.*

---

## How to introduce it (low pressure works best)

The app is built around a simple idea: **the student pulls; the app never pushes.** It never nags, never says "you're behind," never shows a red overdue pile. Introduce it the same way:

- **Offer it, don't assign it.** "Here's a thing some people find useful — try it if you want." Pressure is the fastest way to lose the students who'd benefit most.
- **No "right way" to use it.** Dumping ten random thoughts at midnight is a perfectly good use. So is opening it once a week.
- **Missing days is normal and fine.** The app treats irregular use as completely okay — please do too. There's nothing to "keep up with."
- **Let them find their own features.** There are small hidden things to discover. Don't over-explain; curiosity does the work.

---

## What students will notice (and what it means)

- **A map, not a list.** Thoughts cluster in spaces instead of stacking in a guilt-inducing to-do list.
- **It feels different at different times.** The app quietly senses whether someone's in a focused stretch or a scattered one and gets calmer or fuller to match. It **never names this** or tells the student what "mode" they're in — it just adjusts. There's no quiz and no judgment.
- **Tiny wins, never failures.** Finishing a small thing feels good; not finishing something just lets it gently fade. Nothing piles up as a reminder of falling behind.

If a student asks "is it judging me?" — the honest answer is no. It reflects their own patterns back; it doesn't score them.

---

## What it is NOT

- **Not a medical or diagnostic tool.** It doesn't assess, screen, or label anyone. If a student is struggling in a way that needs real support, this is a helper, not a substitute for a counselor or specialist.
- **Not therapy or advice.** It organizes and reflects; it doesn't counsel.
- **Not a monitoring tool.** Again: you can't see inside it, and it shouldn't be used to check up on anyone.

---

## Setup notes

- **Device:** iPhone or iPad on **iOS 18 or newer**. It runs on older and newer hardware alike — newer devices with Apple Intelligence get slightly smarter sorting, but everything works without it.
- **Permissions are optional and read-only.** It may *offer* to read calendar, reminders, or health (steps/sleep) to show them on the map. A student can say no to all of it and the app still works. Nothing it reads ever leaves the device.
- **Action Button (optional):** on supported iPhones, students can set the side Action Button to capture a thought by voice — handy when writing it down would lose it. Settings ▸ Action Button ▸ Shortcut ▸ Unstuck.
- **No internet required** after install.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I see a student's data or progress?**
No. It's on their device only, with no teacher view — by design.

**Does it collect or sell anything?**
No. No account, no analytics, no cloud. Nothing is collected.

**What if a student stops using it?**
That's fine and expected. It's built for come-and-go use, not streaks.

**A student says it "knows" when they're overwhelmed — is that surveillance?**
No. It reads simple on-device signals (like rhythm and time of day) to gently adjust *its own* look and feel. It never reports anything anywhere.

**Is it only for certain students?**
No. It's for anyone whose head feels too full sometimes. No label needed.

---

*Unstuck keeps a student's mind their own. The best thing you can do is offer it lightly and let them make it theirs.*
